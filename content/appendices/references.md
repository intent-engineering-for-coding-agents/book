# References

*Reviewed: October 25, 2026.*

Grouped by theme, with publication dates for fixed works and review dates for living documentation. Source descriptions identify the claim supported and the kind of evidence. Historical tool releases document the book's dated examples. Entries disclose access limitations.

## Foundations: engineering practice, decision records, plain-text-as-code

- Sam Newman: ["Backends For Frontends"](https://samnewman.io/patterns/architectural/bff/). *November 18, 2015*. Original description of the client-specific backend pattern.
- Andrej Karpathy: ["Vibe coding"](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1886192184808149383). X, *February 2, 2025*. Original post introducing the term.
- Simon Willison: ["Not all AI-assisted programming is vibe coding (but vibe coding rocks)"](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/19/vibe-coding/). *March 19, 2025*. Practitioner commentary distinguishing vibe coding from AI-assisted programming with code review and understanding.
- Ian Sommerville: ["Software Engineering", 10th edition](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/software-engineering/P200000003258?view=educator). Pearson, *2015* (copyright 2016). Textbook treatment of software processes and lifecycle activities.
- David Farley: ["Modern Software Engineering: Doing What Works to Build Better Software Faster"](https://www.informit.com/store/modern-software-engineering-doing-what-works-to-build-9780137314744). Addison-Wesley, *2021*. Engineering principles and feedback. With Jez Humble: ["Continuous Delivery: Reliable Software Releases through Build, Test, and Deployment Automation"](https://www.informit.com/store/continuous-delivery-reliable-software-releases-through-9780321770424), Addison-Wesley, *2010*. Deployment pipelines and delivery feedback. The associated [continuousdelivery.com](https://continuousdelivery.com/) site defines continuous delivery (reviewed October 21, 2026).
- Kent Beck: ["Test-Driven Development: By Example"](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780321146533/samplepages/0321146530.pdf). Addison-Wesley, *2002* (copyright 2003). Original book on the test-first cycle and red-green-refactor practice.
- Dan North: ["Introducing BDD"](https://dannorth.net/blog/introducing-bdd/). First published in *Better Software*, *March 2006*, republished online September 20, 2006. Original account of behavior-driven development and executable acceptance criteria.
- Ron Jeffries: ["Essential XP: Card, Conversation, Confirmation"](https://ronjeffries.com/xprog/articles/expcardconversationconfirmation). *August 30, 2001*. Original essay describing a user story as a card that tokens a requirement, a conversation that carries its detail, and a confirmation through acceptance tests.
- Mike Cohn: ["User Story Template: What It Is and Why It Works So Well"](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/blog/why-the-three-part-user-story-template-works-so-well). Mountain Goat Software (reviewed October 25, 2026, page shows last updated April 22, 2026). Author-maintained description of the who, what, and why template, its attribution to Rachel Davies at Connextra, and the role of the "so that" clause. The page quotes Cohn saying he described the template in *User Stories Applied* as "As a (role), I want (function) so that (business value)", without a chapter or page number. Seb Rose (listed below) independently says the book includes the template. This review could not confirm the exact wording in the book, so the wording rests on this page.
- Mike Cohn: ["User Stories Applied: For Agile Software Development"](https://www.informit.com/title/0321205685). Addison-Wesley, *March 1, 2004*. Book on user stories. This review read the publisher's free sample pages (front matter, Introduction, Chapter 3, index) and an [archived copy of the author's free Chapter 2, "Writing Stories"](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/uploads/articles/User-Stories-Applied-Mike-Cohn.pdf) (original address returned 404 on October 25, 2026). The Introduction says stories keep just enough written down to prompt conversation, and Chapter 2 says details settled in conversation become tests. The remaining chapters were not read.
- Agile Alliance: ["User Story Template"](https://www.agilealliance.org/glossary/user-story-template/) (reviewed October 25, 2026). Primary glossary entry dating the role, feature, and reason format to Connextra in 2001, and describing it as an aid more than a rule.
- Seb Rose: ["User stories and BDD (part #1): the origins and evolution of the user story"](https://cucumber.io/blog/bdd/user-stories-are-not-the-same-as-features/). Cucumber blog, *October 31, 2019*. Practitioner account crediting the template to Rachel Davies in the early 2000s and its spread to Cohn's books *Agile Estimating and Planning* and *User Stories Applied*. The post gives no publication years for those books.
- Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, and David S. Duncan: ["Know Your Customers' 'Jobs to Be Done'"](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done). *Harvard Business Review*, *September 2016* (reprint R1609D). - Paul Hammant: [trunkbaseddevelopment.com](https://trunkbaseddevelopment.com/) (reviewed October 21, 2026) and ["Trunk-Based Development and Branch By Abstraction"](https://leanpub.com/trunk-based-development), Leanpub (available by *2020*).
